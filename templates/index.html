A maiden voyage across the Atlantic

From Southampton to the open sea

Titanic left Southampton on 10 April 1912, called at Cherbourg and Queenstown, then turned west toward New York. Shortly before midnight on 14 April, she struck an iceberg in the North Atlantic. The ship sank in the early hours of 15 April.

Photographs & memory

A liner, then a wreck

Archival images hold two moments more than seven decades apart: the ship setting out from Southampton, and its bow resting on the ocean floor. Titanic was discovered in 1985, roughly 3,800 metres below the surface.

The shipwreck

A site of history and remembrance

The wreck lies in two main sections on the seabed, about 600 kilometres southeast of Newfoundland. Its discovery gave researchers a way to study the ship and the disaster, while the site remains a grave for those who were lost.

More than 1,500 people died. The exact totals vary across historical accounts; the passenger model below is a learning tool based on a passenger dataset, and its estimate does not represent any individual’s real fate.
